### 1.2 Where we stand, critically

Weaker than comparators:
- **No physical flight at all.** Crazyswarm (49 vehicles), Vásárhelyi (30 outdoor), Zhou (10 in a forest), SwarmNxt (6 indoors, 2 h) and every commercial show have real-world data. Our evidence ceiling is SITL.
- **Safety is empirical.** Our separation guard is a sampled, local, velocity-aware rule. CBF safety filters (Ames; Wang) give an invariance proof under stated assumptions. EGO-Swarm and Crazyswarm-style planners check trajectories, not just states. The repo's own review found the old additive guard could be overridden (fixed in `17ad625`), which illustrates the risk of rule-based safety without a proof.
- **Separation is not scaled to vehicle size.** Our gate is 2.5 m. We have not stated a vehicle size or a downwash model. SwarmNxt reports 0.65 m minimum on 0.27 m vehicles; the ratio is only meaningful once our assumed vehicle size is fixed.
- **No downwash or wind model**, whereas the Crazyswarm line of work treats downwash explicitly.
- **Hybrid fallback holds formation but does not continue the mission** (review 3.5); Skybrush has defined fleet failsafes (Land, RTH, Halt) and a secondary radio; ArduPilot Follow has a defined timeout-and-hold behaviour.

Comparable or stronger:
- **Architecture comparison in one codebase** (centralized, decentralized, hybrid, same safety layer, paired seeds, 30-seed statistics) is something most comparators do not offer; they each pick one architecture.
- **Authenticated peer links (HMAC), leases, clock sync and failure injection** are more than ArduPilot Follow provides, and similar in spirit to Skybrush's fleet management.
- **Tooling** (dashboard, record, 7 formations, transition protocol with prepare/ack/go/HOLD) is competitive for an FYP and visible in a demo.
- **Honest negative results** (the review documents failures and fixes) are a strength for a thesis.

## 2. Standards and regulations

Rule of thumb: for a **lab simulation** none of these are legally binding. They matter in the thesis as (a) the framework a real deployment would sit in, and (b) a source of requirements (separation, comms integrity, failsafes) to cite. For **real flight** the national authority (CAA/EASA/FAA) and the SORA authorisation are what bind you; no swarm-specific standard exists.

### Design notes for in-flight mode switching (#2)

- **Single authority and epochs.** Each switch carries a monotonically increasing epoch; agents ignore stale epochs. This is what stops mixed-mode drift observed in the 10-drone hybrid runs (review 3.4).
- **Prepare/ack/common-start.** Reuse the transition protocol: coordinator proposes, collects acks within a deadline, announces a start time `t0` on the shared clock with a margin larger than worst-case latency plus clock error (#10).
- **Bumpless transfer.** At `t0`, seed the new controller with the current commanded velocity and last accelerations; blend `u = (1-a(t))*u_old + a(t)*u_new`, with `a` smooth (e.g. cubic) and the safety filter (#1) applied after blending so safety never depends on which controller is active.
- **Rollback.** Abort conditions: missing acks, margin below a threshold, or tracking error blow-up during the blend. Rollback target is the previous mode (or HOLD, as in your transition protocol). Rollback must also be epoch-tagged.
- **Dwell.** No new switch before ramp time plus settling time, as in your hybrid hysteresis.
- **Test.** Scenario matrix: switch with 0, 10, 30% packet loss; coordinator killed during prepare; an agent killed during ramp.

### Things NOT worth doing in 8 weeks

- Learned / RL swarm controllers (e.g. arXiv 2109.07735, 2309.13285): large effort, needs training infrastructure, hard to explain safety. 
- Full EGO-Swarm-style perception and mapping stack: needs obstacles, VIO, depth sensing; outside scope.
- Claiming or attempting hardware flight, ESP32/CAN link, or Remote ID implementation this term. A document-only readiness note (#14) is enough.
- Proving flocking stability for the modified controller in full generality; give a scoped argument (consensus part, dwell time) plus empirical evidence instead.
- PTP-grade time sync or RTK modelling.
- A second GUI or new simulator. Spend Gazebo time on a short, scripted, recorded run with about 6 drones, since it runs at about 0.2x real time.
- Large-scale (50+ drone) claims; 10 is what you can defend.
- Reproducing ORCA/RVO from scratch unless it is the CBF alternative you choose; one of the two (CBF or ORCA) is enough, and CBF has the stronger story.
